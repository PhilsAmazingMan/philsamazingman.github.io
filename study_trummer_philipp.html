Using AI chatbots to provide self-help depression interventions for university students: A randomized trial of effectiveness

This page summarizes a study by Liu et al. (2022), which tested whether an AI chatbot called XiaoNan could provide an effective self-help depression intervention for university students.

Study overview

Design

Randomized controlled trial

The study used a randomized controlled trial design. Participants were assigned either to a chatbot intervention group or to a bibliotherapy control group.

Population

University students

The study included 83 Chinese full-time university students aged 19 to 28 years. Of these, 31 were undergraduate students and 52 were postgraduate students. All participants had elevated depression symptoms at baseline.

Comparison

Chatbot vs. bibliotherapy

The chatbot group used XiaoNan, while the control group received a minimal bibliotherapy intervention based on a CBT self-help book.

What was XiaoNan?

Chatbot intervention

A CBT-based therapy chatbot

XiaoNan was designed to deliver self-help support for depression. Its content was based on cognitive behavioral therapy principles and reviewed by professional therapists.

Interaction

Text and voice messages

XiaoNan supported both text and voice input. Voice messages were first converted into text before being processed by the chatbot.

What did the intervention include?

Daily support

Emotion tracking

Users could use XiaoNan to track daily emotions and receive empathic responses from the chatbot.

CBT content

Working with thoughts and emotions

XiaoNan aimed to help users separate emotions, thoughts, reactions, and behaviors, and to develop new automatic thoughts.

Safety

Emergency support

Participants were provided with a local mental assistance hotline. Professionals could intervene by telephone when emergency psychological assistance was needed.

What outcomes were measured?

Depression

PHQ-9

Depression symptoms were measured using the Patient Health Questionnaire-9.

Anxiety

GAD-7

Anxiety symptoms were measured using the Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 scale.

Affect and experience

PANAS, CSQ-8, WAI-SR

The study also measured positive and negative affect, user satisfaction, therapeutic alliance, adherence, and open-ended user feedback.

Main findings

Depression
d = 0.83

In the intention-to-treat analysis, the chatbot group showed a significantly greater reduction in depression symptoms compared with the bibliotherapy group.

Anxiety
d = 0.30

Anxiety symptoms also decreased significantly, although the effect size was smaller than for depression.

Therapeutic alliance
higher

The chatbot group reported a stronger therapeutic alliance than the bibliotherapy group, suggesting that conversational AI may support a sense of working relationship.

User feedback

Positive feedback

Accessible, friendly, educational

Users described XiaoNan as easy to access, empathic or friendly, interesting, and educational. Some users also valued the depression-related information and interactive CBT elements.

Negative feedback

Impersonal and repetitive

Users also reported limitations. Some described the chatbot as impersonal, unnatural, rigid, repetitive, too general, or sometimes misunderstanding user input.

Why is this study relevant?

This study is relevant because it tested an AI chatbot under a longer 16-week follow-up period and compared it with an established self-help approach rather than with no intervention.
Contribution

Evidence under pragmatic conditions

The authors aimed to add evidence for chatbot therapy as a convenient, affordable, and interactive self-help intervention for depression.

Connection to the meta-analysis

Example of AI-based mental health support

The study illustrates how AI-based conversational agents can be used to deliver structured mental health support and how user experience may influence the success of such interventions.

Limitations and critical perspective

The authors noted several limitations. The chatbot content was limited and could become repetitive. The study focused only on depression, although users may experience multiple mental health problems at the same time. The participant sample also lacked diversity, so future studies should test the intervention in broader populations and over longer periods.

Key takeaway

The XiaoNan study suggests that chatbot-delivered self-help interventions can reduce depression symptoms among university students and may create a stronger therapeutic alliance than bibliotherapy. However, the study also shows that chatbot quality, personalization, content variety, and user experience are central for long-term engagement and effectiveness.

Source: Liu, H., Peng, H., Song, X., Xu, C. & Zhang, M. (2022). Using AI chatbots to provide self-help depression interventions for university students: A randomized trial of effectiveness. Internet Interventions, 27, 100495.
